Unveiling Biosensing & Diagnostics

A pioneering lab, led from the bench

Dr. Adewoyin M. Ogunmolasuyi is an emerging African researcher with global ambitions to deliver innovations in medical diagnostics, working across microfluidics and biosensing.

During his doctoral studies, he constructed a microfluidic paper analytical device (µPAD) — of similar geometry to a lateral flow device — to deliver aptamer-tethered enzyme capture (APTEC) and enzyme-linked oligonucleotide assays for detecting Plasmodium falciparum lactate dehydrogenase in malaria.

  • Employed several electrochemical biosensor designs including electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS), cyclic voltammetry (CV), and chronoamperometry
  • Awarded international research funding via USAID through the African Research Network for Neglected Tropical Diseases (ARNTD) for low-cost diagnostic tests
  • Built an international consortium with the University of Glasgow and Rhodes University, with collaborator networks across Nigeria

How we work

From consultation to deployment

A typical engagement, whether you're a diagnostics company, a research partner, or a student.

01

Consult

We start with a call or in-lab meeting to understand the diagnostic problem, target pathogen, and deployment setting.

02

Design

Assay and device architecture is drafted — choice of biosensor format, aptamer or antibody target, and microfluidic layout.

03

Prototype

We fabricate a working device in-house and run it against electrochemical and molecular benchmarks (EIS, CV, PCR, LAMP).

04

Validate

Sensitivity, specificity, and stability are tested under field-representative conditions, not just bench conditions.

05

Deploy

Handover, training, and — where needed — ongoing technical support for diagnostics companies and field teams.

What we do

Areas we excel in most

Biosensor Training

Fabrication of colorimetric, electrochemical, and molecular diagnostics — training the next generation of diagnostic professionals.

Pathogen Analysis

In-depth molecular and microbiology analysis via PCR, loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), and recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA).
